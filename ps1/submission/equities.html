PS1 — Equities: intraday descriptive statistics¶

Two Nasdaq-listed stocks at opposite ends of the size distribution, over one full regular session (2026-09-02, 09:30–16:00 ET):

ticker price on the day market cap
mega cap NVDA $219 → $224 ~$5.5T
micro cap GPRO $1.22 → $1.71 (+40%) ~$0.27B

The source is Databento MBO for XNAS.ITCH — every add, cancel, fill and trade message Nasdaq published, one row each. mbo.py replays those messages into a full limit order book and hands back three objects per symbol:

  • trades — the trade tape, signed, with the quote prevailing at each print
  • quotes — the BBO, one row per change
  • grid — the book sampled at the end of every second, plus resting depth by distance from the mid

Parts (a)–(k) are computed by hfstats.py, which is written once against these objects and is also what the currencies notebook runs, so the two panels are measured with one definition of every statistic.

Three things about the raw feed worth stating up front, because they shape the numbers below:

  1. An execution emits three records — T (the trade), F (the fill against the resting order) and C (which removes the shares). Only A and C move the book; counting F as well would double the depth removed, and counting it as a trade would double the volume.
  2. T.side is the aggressor, except for non-displayed executions and the cross, which arrive unsigned (22% of NVDA prints). Those are signed with the quote rule.
  3. The book has to be built from the pre-market. Databento snapshots the book only at UTC midnight, so the request runs from there (20:00 ET the evening before, when Nasdaq's book is empty) to the close and the pre-market is replayed before the first 09:30 snapshot; only the regular session is recorded. Started from an empty book at 09:30 instead, 0.3% of NVDA's and 0.8% of GPRO's session cancels reference an order never seen — resting pre-open orders, which are not re-added after the cross — and the resting depth within $0.50 of the mid is understated by 7% (NVDA) and 37% (GPRO) at 09:31, still 2% and 22% at 10:00, and 0.2% and 8% on average over the day. Touch depth and spreads are unaffected for NVDA, and GPRO's touch by <1%. With the full-day replay every session cancel references an order seen added.

Choice of stocks¶

Size shows up in the data long before we reach the statistics: NVDA trades ~12x as often and turns over ~180x the dollars, but GPRO's quoted spread is ~93x wider in relative terms. GPRO also happened to rally 40% on this day, from $1.22 to $1.71 in a steady climb between noon and 15:30 — worth keeping in mind wherever a GPRO statistic is quoted in basis points of a moving price. The opening cross is excluded from the tape throughout (hfstats.tape): it prints once, unsigned, and is ~30x a normal trade, so left in it dominates the first minute of every trade statistic.

Part (f) — depth within twice the day's average spread¶

Shares resting within one average spread either side of the mid, i.e. inside a band of twice the day's mean quoted spread. The best bid sits half a spread from the mid, so the band is empty exactly when the prevailing spread exceeds twice its average — frac_empty reports how often that happens. Note the degenerate case: if a name's spread is always one tick, the band reaches no further than the BBO and (f) just reproduces the depth from (e). GPRO — one tick wide in 99.6% of seconds — is exactly that case; NVDA's 1.7-cent average spread puts the band one level past the touch.

Part (g) — 5-second price impact per minute¶

Within each minute, regress the 5-second midquote return following each print on the sign of that print:

$$10^4 \cdot \log\!\big(m_{t+5s}/m_t\big) \;=\; \alpha + \lambda \, s_t + \varepsilon_t, \qquad s_t \in \{-1, +1\}$$

$\lambda$ is the midquote move a buy pushes through, in basis points. Because $s_t$ is binary the slope is just the difference in mean response between buys and sells, halved — computed in closed form rather than by looping OLS over 390 minutes. Minutes with fewer than ten prints, or with trades on one side only, cannot identify it and are left blank.

Part (j) — realized variance¶

$RV = \sum_t r_t^2$ over the session, for each series and sampling frequency. Gaps between the midquote and transaction figures, and between the two frequencies, are where microstructure noise shows up — the finer the sampling, and the closer to the traded price rather than the mid, the more of the variance is noise rather than signal.

Volatility signature¶

RV of each series against the interval it is sampled at, in bps². For a price contaminated by bid–ask bounce this curve falls as the interval lengthens; the share of intervals with no price change shows how much of the high-frequency figure is instead being held down by discreteness (the currencies notebook finds that effect dominates for EBS mids).

In [16]:
_freqs = ["1s", "2s", "5s", "10s", "30s", MINUTE, "2min", "5min"]


def _signature(series):
    """RV in bps^2, and the share of intervals with no price change, by frequency."""
    out = {}
    for fq in _freqs:
        r = np.log(series.resample(fq).last().ffill().dropna()).diff().dropna()
        out[fq] = {"RV (bps^2)": 1e8 * (r ** 2).sum(), "zero returns (%)": 100 * (r == 0).mean()}
    return pd.DataFrame(out).T


pd.concat({(s, k): _signature(src) for s, d in days.items()
           for k, src in (("mid", d.grid.mid), ("trade", tape(d).price))},
          axis=1, names=["symbol", "series", ""]).round(1)

Part (k) — autocorrelation of returns¶

The Box–Pierce $Q$ statistic tests whether the first $k$ autocorrelations are jointly zero; the ACF and partial ACF show where any dependence sits. Two things to read off: whether transaction returns are more negatively autocorrelated at lag 1 than midquote returns (the bid-ask bounce, which only bites when sampling is fast enough that consecutive observations straddle the spread), and whether the sign flips between the one-second and one-minute clocks.

Observations¶

  1. Size sets the spread, but not the dollar depth. NVDA quotes 1.7 cents wide (0.76 bps) and GPRO 1.0 cent (71 bps): a 93x gap in relative terms that is almost entirely the tick. GPRO sits at the one-cent minimum in 99.6% of seconds, so its spread in basis points is just the inverse of its price — 85 bps in the first five minutes at $1.22, 59 bps in the last at $1.70 — and the "narrowing" through the day in the spread figure is the rally, not a liquidity change. NVDA is one tick wide only 41% of the time, so its spread does carry information. Depth at the touch is where the tick-constraint prediction bites: GPRO rests 50,700 shares at the BBO against NVDA's 462 — but in dollars that is $73k against $104k, the same order of magnitude, not "far thinner". A one-cent spread on a $1.50 stock is such a wide gate that the queue behind it is long; the band measure (f) shows the same thing, since for GPRO the ±1-spread band never reaches past the touch. Volume tells the size story more honestly: $5.1bn against $28mm on the day, 892 against 75 prints a minute, 14 displayed orders per trade against 5, and a cancel-to-add ratio of about one for both — nearly every displayed order is cancelled rather than filled. NVDA's tape is 74% odd lots (median print 25 shares); GPRO's median print is a round lot.
  2. Impact is larger for GPRO in basis points and smaller in cents. Pooled over the session with HAC errors, λ is 0.46 bps for NVDA (t = 28) and 26.7 bps for GPRO (t = 28); the per-minute means are 0.33 and 22.7 bps, lower because thin minutes weigh as much as busy ones. In price units that is 1.0 cent for NVDA — a signed trade moves the mid by about one tick, roughly 0.6 of its spread, within five seconds — and 0.4 cents for GPRO, less than half a tick. GPRO's mid can only move in half-cent steps, so 26.7 bps is the probability that a trade shifts the touch times the 29–41 bps that one half-tick is worth at its price; its λ falls through the afternoon in the figure for the same mechanical reason its spread does. NVDA's λ is highest in the first five minutes (0.55 bps) with spikes to 1.3 at 09:55 and 1.2 at 11:00, and lowest into the close (0.24).
  3. Bounce is weak in both names; what shows up instead is discreteness and a transitory component at one minute. For NVDA, transaction RV at one second exceeds midquote RV by only 1.8% (33,019 against 32,435 bps²), and the lag-1 autocorrelation of one-second returns is positive for both series (+0.017 trade, +0.025 mid, Box–Pierce p < 0.01): with 15 prints a second, mostly odd lots inside or at a 1.7-cent spread, the last trade of each second is essentially the touch, and what the ACF picks up is gradual quote adjustment, not Roll's bounce. The volatility signature is two-sided: the midquote RV rises from 32,400 bps² at one second to 35,000 at thirty seconds (21% of one-second mid returns are exactly zero, so discreteness holds the fine-grid figure down, as it does for the EBS mids) and then falls to 27,300 at one minute and 22,300 at five, where a transitory component averages out. That component is visible directly: the lag-1 autocorrelation of one-minute returns is −0.12 for the mid and −0.12 for trades (p = 0.02), negative in nine of thirteen half hours and −0.51 in 11:30–12:00, and it is in the mid, so it is not bid–ask bounce. GPRO is the discreteness case: 97% of its one-second midquote returns and 94% of its transaction returns are zero, yet its RV falls monotonically from 2.83 million bps² at one second to 2.40 million at thirty seconds and is flat after that, its one-second returns are negatively autocorrelated in both series (−0.02, p < 0.001), and trade RV exceeds mid RV at every frequency — the standard bounce signature, faint but there, from a print that alternates between a bid and an ask one cent apart. Its one-minute returns show no autocorrelation at all (p = 0.45). The session RV is 1.8% for NVDA and 16.8% for GPRO — a 40% day.
  4. The U is half a U. NVDA's opening half hour is the extreme of everything: spread 1.8 bps in the first five minutes against 0.66 at noon, depth 200 shares against 360, and the variance of one-minute returns 237 bps² against 15 in the 15:00 half hour — sixteen times. But there is no second arm. Spread and impact fall monotonically to their lows in the last five minutes (0.53 bps, 0.24 bps) while depth triples to 1,410 shares and the busiest minute of the day is 15:59 with 675k shares: the closing auction attracts resting liquidity and volume without widening anything, the opposite of the open. The variance ticks up only slightly in the last half hour (30 against 15 bps²). The classic pattern is a U in volume and an L in cost. GPRO's day is shaped by its own news rather than the clock: half-hour variance peaks at 12,900 bps² in 13:30–14:00, during the rally, not at the open (10,600), and the largest one-minute move (+4.3%) is at 13:55. While it trends the lag-1 autocorrelation of its one-minute returns is positive in most half hours (+0.22, +0.16, +0.20), the same momentum-while-trending sign the EUR/USD series shows going into the FOMC release. Its cancel-to-add ratio is 1.03 for the day; NVDA's is 0.97, with the 09:30 minute alone carrying 160k add messages against a median of 9,900 — the fleeting-liquidity burst is an opening phenomenon.
